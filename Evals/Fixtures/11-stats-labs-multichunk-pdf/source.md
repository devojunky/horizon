## Course description
An introduction to statistical reasoning with real data. Topics include exploratory data analysis, probability, sampling distributions, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests, and regression. Computation is done in R; no prior programming experience is assumed. Each week pairs a lecture with a hands-on lab in which you analyze a dataset and write up findings.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the course you will be able to summarize a dataset numerically and graphically, state and check the assumptions of a statistical procedure, compute and interpret confidence intervals and p-values, fit and diagnose a simple linear regression, and communicate a quantitative analysis in writing to a non-specialist reader.

## Weekly topics
Week 1 (Jan 26, 28): What is data? Variables, cases, the R environment.
Week 2 (Feb 2, 4): Visualizing distributions; histograms, boxplots, scatterplots.
Week 3 (Feb 9, 11): Numerical summaries; mean, median, spread, outliers.
Week 4 (Feb 16, 18): Probability rules; conditional probability; independence.
Week 5 (Feb 23, 25): Random variables; binomial and normal distributions.
Week 6 (Mar 2, 4): Sampling distributions; the central limit theorem.
Week 7 (Mar 9, 11): Midterm; confidence intervals for a mean.
Week 8 (Mar 23, 25): Hypothesis testing logic; p-values and errors.
Week 9 (Mar 30, Apr 1): Comparing two groups; paired designs.
Week 10 (Apr 6, 8): Correlation and simple linear regression.
Week 11 (Apr 13, 15): Regression inference and diagnostics.
Week 12 (Apr 20, 22): Categorical data; chi-square tests.
Week 13 (Apr 27, 29): Multiple regression preview; project work.
Week 14 (May 4, 6): Review; project presentations in lab.
