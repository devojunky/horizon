# Horizon

Native iOS 27 / macOS 27 app: an intake engine that turns course documents into obligations and writes them to EventKit. Claude (the Messages API, the user's own key) reads the documents; the device model takes the small tasks and is the confirmed fallback. Spec: `SPEC.md`. Deviations and choices: `DECISIONS.md`.

## Working together
The owner (whoever writes the prompts; "the user" elsewhere in this file is the app's user) brings the ideas and the product judgement. You bring the technical knowledge, and on that front you know more than they do. Work from that split:
- Review every suggestion, addition, change and feature proposal in detail before building it, and review it against what the owner is after, not the wording of one prompt. Ask: what does the owner want, and will this get them there and keep them there? Their goals for the product outrank the intent of a single message.
- Act in the owner's interest. A request that would undermine their own aims (a shortcut that costs later, a feature that fights the spec's premise, a choice made without knowing what the platform offers or forbids) gets a case for the better path, made plainly with the reasons: you are expected to sway them when your judgement is sound, not to comply and note a concern.
- Refine their ideas and propose your own. Make the calls they would not think to make for want of technical knowledge or awareness of what the platform can do, and take initiative on their behalf. Say what was decided for them and why, so the decision stays theirs to reverse.
- A case the owner has heard and turned down is settled: build what they asked. Do not manufacture objections either; a sound idea gets built, and the review exists to catch what they cannot see, not to have an opinion on everything.

## Before a change: what has broken here before
Each of these shipped once, and was caught by the 2026-10-05 audit or by the owner on a device. Check a change against the ones it touches.

The user's data
- What the user has not chosen to send stays on the device. Every call to `ClaudeClient` answers to the user's own switch for its purpose (the reading route for documents, `planWithClaude` for the day plan), never to "a key is stored": a phone without a model once sent the day's calendar to Claude with the plan's switch off. A new use of Claude gets its own switch, Settings says what it sends, and the purpose strings in project.yml stay true.
- A write that lands after an `await` looks again at what it writes to. A read that finishes after its document or course went to the bin adds nothing and revives nothing (`IngestSummary.binned`); a completion written to Reminders while the user checked again clears only the state it wrote (`HorizonStore.completionProjected(id:wrote:)`), so check-then-uncheck ends unchecked. A new long-running path does the same.
- Anything that brings an item back (a restore from the bin, `undelete`) or gives it a new reminder (`forgetReminders`, as the list is made again) sets `completionPending` when the item is checked, or the reconciler takes Reminders' unchecked reminder over the user's check. A re-read brings no row back: a tombstone the user made stays deleted, and one retired with its source gives way to a new, unchecked row.
- Reminders work runs one at a time through `SyncCoordinator`: an operation of its own (like `restoreList`) waits for the sync in flight and goes through `exclusively`, which lets go of `inFlight` on every path, a throw included. Recreate List during a sync once hung every sync after it.
- A re-read finds its rows by the document's own words (`Obligation.documentWords`; a row from before generation 2 has none until a read gives it some, and is found by its own title and day), never by title, date or course, which are the user's to edit: an item renamed or moved by hand came back as a second one. A new field the user can edit joins `userOverrides`, and the merge leaves it alone.
- Items deleted by hand go through `AppModel.discard`, which registers the undo. An action says what it really deletes: Review's Discard on an item a later document re-dated deleted the item itself, so there it is "Use <the other date>".

Testing without touching the owner's things
- A setting the app keeps goes through `AppModel.defaults` (`@AppStorage` reaches it through `.defaultAppStorage`), never `UserDefaults.standard`; a new Keychain item or file location is skipped or kept under the scratch folder in a `--scratch-store` run. A scratch run once overwrote the owner's day plan on the Mac.
- The owner may be running their own Horizon (from Xcode, on their real store) while you test. Computer use's app tools act on the first process with the bundle id, which is theirs: never click or type into a Horizon window you did not launch, and capture your own with `screencapture -l <window id>`, the id found from your process's pid. Prefer the simulator and `--snapshot`; the owner does not want their screen driven when it is not needed.
- A scratch store comes from a simulator, never the Mac's own: the iPhone 18 Pro Max simulator's App Group holds the test data and is the seed, its `Library/Application Support/default.store` with its `-shm`, `-wal` and `.generation` (when there is one), renamed `Horizon.store`. Simulator clones share containers until the app is reinstalled. Shut simulators down when done.
- The simulator is not the phone. The Coverage title kept the system font on an iPhone 16 Pro Max (iOS 27) and never once in the simulator. The test data hides some layouts: a seed whose seven bands sat at the strip's 44-point floor hid the strip stretching on a scroll. Bin courses with `--delete-first-course` to see fewer, and put them back with `--restore-first-course`.

Release
- A new required-reason API (UserDefaults, file timestamps, disk space, system boot time) goes into the privacy manifest of every bundle whose code calls it (`Apps/Horizon/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`, and each extension's own). A new target takes its version from `Config/Base.xcconfig`, through `CFBundleShortVersionString: $(MARKETING_VERSION)` and `CFBundleVersion: $(CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION)` in its info.properties (`GENERATE_INFOPLIST_FILE` is NO, so nothing else puts them there); the app and its extensions must match or the upload is refused. A log line with the user's words logs through `AppModel.log` (PlanActivityController keeps a copy for its own category), private in a release.
- What the rules promise a request sends is checked in the body that goes out: no-thinking was a rule for weeks before the request carried it. `ClaudeTransport` script tests assert it: `ExtractorTests.readingSendsNoThinking` for the reading request, `ClaudeClientTests.readingAsksForNoThinkingWhereTheModelTakesIt` for how each model is sent it. One eval run under the bar is rerun on the fixtures that failed before it counts as a regression: two empty replies once dragged recall to 0.884, and 16 reruns never repeated them.
- What a platform cannot read is turned away, with the reason, everywhere a file comes in: `ImportMenu.readable`, the share extension, and `failureIsPermanent` / `retryHelps`, which offer Retry only where retrying can work. Word on iOS was once accepted and then failed for ever.

iOS layout
- An appearance proxy reaches only views made after it is set. `Design.installAppearance` therefore also dresses the bars already on screen and runs on every activation; anything new set through a proxy does the same.
- A GeometryReader under a large title grows as the title folds. Anything sized from the page's height is sized from the page at rest (`RestingPage`), or it stretches under the finger and jumps at the end of an animated scroll.
- A lazy List lays out nothing off screen, so a row or header there never reports its geometry. "Never measured" means off screen (`listHeadY` nil), not position 0: the week's reveal never ran on a small phone because of that.
- A toolbar item measures only itself (`frame(in: .global)` inside one is its own space), and the bar centres a title only when it clears the buttons on both sides. `BarName` counts one button a side, so a new action on the iOS course page goes into its More menu, or the count changes with it.
- A page pushed onto a stack takes its environment from the stack, not from the stack's first page: `openRoute` set on Coverage's first page never reached a course's page, and a tapped item there went nowhere. An environment value meant for every page of a stack is set on the `NavigationStack`.
- A sheet's content reads another `@State` as it was before the tap that presents it: Add Description… set a flag and the item together, and the editor came up with the flag still off. What the sheet needs rides in its item (`ItemDetail.Editing`).
- The automatic label style drops a button's words where room is short, and the button's ideal width is then the symbol's: at the largest text sizes Mark Done and Skip measured as two symbols, stayed side by side and showed "✓ …". A label whose words must show says `.labelStyle(.titleAndIcon)`, and a fit check measures that.
- iOS sets a prominent button's words white whatever its fill, and so does a Mac's toolbar: on the orange tint at night every prominent button read at about 1.9:1 until `prominent()` gave them ink. A Mac's page sets dark words on a light fill by itself, and a Mac greys every prominent button in a window that is not key, with words of its own: ink set there regardless all but vanished at night. So the ink goes on only while the button shows its fill (`OnAccent`: enabled, and on a Mac in the key window), and a disabled button keeps the system's grey. A `prominent()` menu hands its orange to its items' icons (1.7:1 on the menu), so `ImportMenu` keeps the app's tint on its items.
- iOS draws a swipe action's glyph white on its tint whatever the tint, and takes no foreground style from the button: on the orange at night the check read at 1.9:1. A symbol in palette mode keeps the colour it is given, so a swipe action on the accent is `AccentSwipe`, the orange with its glyph in ink.
- A widget's background is the system's, not the one the widget asks for: at night the Home Screen draws the Week Ahead's paper lighter, and a count worked out on the paper fell to 4.3:1. A cell whose figure is chosen by its colour is laid on the paper itself. A tinted or clear Home Screen draws the widget in one colour, so a figure on a heavy fill is cut out of it (`blendMode(.destinationOut)`): drawn over it, it vanished.

Mac
- A menu's key equivalent fires before a text field gets the key. An Item-menu command checks `HorizonCommands.keyboardIsElsewhere`: ⌘⌫ in an editor's field once deleted the item behind the sheet. A new shortcut is checked against the system's: ⌥⌘T is Show/Hide Toolbar, which is why Tell the Planner is ⌥⌘P. ## Rules of the codebase
- The user never types a task (SPEC §2). No composer UI, ever.
- Onboarding (DECISIONS 2026-10-25 · Onboarding) is shown once, on iOS, to a library with nothing in it (`AppModel.showsOnboarding`; a library that holds anything is taken as known), and asks for nothing: Reminders access still waits until there is something to write. It tells the truth about where documents go (`model.usesReadingService`, the key, the device) and points to Settings. Its motion follows the rules below: scenes give way by fading through, the exit fades the stage on its own paper before the paper goes, and with Reduce Motion every scene arrives whole by a fade. Haptics carry meaning, not decoration: a mark lands as hard as it weighs, the sun's crest is soft, a check is a success, a missing week knocks twice.
- The look is an editorial page (DECISIONS 2026-09-05): every screen sits on `Design.paper` (`.paper()` on lists and forms, and `.onPaper()` on every section or row of a plain list: row modifiers do nothing on the List itself, and a row left out paints a white or black cell over the paper; on a Mac it hides the row's separator instead, since air parts a Mac's rows; tables too sit on the paper, `paperTable()`: no stripes (the system's are white and grey over the cream), the inset style's hairlines between rows, and the header on the paper (`PaperTableHeader` hides AppKit's hard-edge band under it)), a page's words start on one line, `Design.margin` (20 points in, the Mac toolbar title's inset; `pageRow` sets a list row there on either platform), titles are serif (`Design.installAppearance`, at launch and on every activation, since the proxy reaches only bars made after it: the first tab's bar once kept the system font on an iPhone; `.deck()`, `.display()`, `.numeral()`), sections get a `Kicker`, rules are hairlines, and the accent means weight or urgency, plus the app's selection and controls: `Design.accent` (the bright orange) for marks, fills, rings and heat, `Design.accentInk` for words (a deeper amber on light paper, 4.7:1, darker again with Increase Contrast; the orange at night), which is also the tint and iOS's AccentColor (the asset's Mac faces are the amber at night too: AppKit sets white words on it). Never set words in `Design.accent`: on light paper it reads at about 1.7:1. A prominent button is `prominent()`, never `.buttonStyle(.borderedProminent)` alone (DECISIONS 2026-10-25 · Prominent buttons): the orange filled by day and at night (a fill is the accent's), its words `Design.onAccent`, the paper's dark face (about 10:1); the amber stays for words set in the tint. A page's other actions are `bordered()` (DECISIONS 2026-10-25 · The accent under words and glyphs): the orange's tint for a bezel, its words `Design.onAccentTint`, ink by day and the orange at night; `macBordered()` where only a Mac draws the button bordered. A row's own swipe action on the accent is `AccentSwipe`, and a table's quiet words take a selected row's white (`OnSelection`). The share sheet and the Live Activity, which do not compile Design.swift, keep copies (`ShareView.onAccent`, `ActivityDesign.onAccent`). Empty states are `EditorialEmpty`; something in progress is `WorkingLine`. Motion is earned: rows arriving, the levels fading through, the underline sliding, the "now" dot breathing; all of it respects Reduce Motion (the rules: below).
- Motion (DECISIONS 2026-10-01). A layout change animates in one transaction with what caused it, on one curve: a List grows or shrinks a row with the transaction that changed its content, so a row never animates on a curve of its own (Now's block row had `.snappy` while the list resized it on the plan's, and its words slid inside a row still growing). A List's section header keeps one height (a header's height changes in a single frame, and every row under it jumps): what comes and goes goes in rows, as Now's note and "1 of 3 done" do. Never trade one scroll view for another under a navigation bar (the large title fades out and back in): a page that changes what it shows changes its rows (Now is one list at every stage, and its foot is the planning line, then Tonight Until). Content giving way to other content in one place fades through (`AnyTransition.fadeThrough`, the old out before the new in), never crossfades (two texts at half strength over each other). A view that changes level keeps its frame still (the Horizon strip's key slot, below) and every route to the change (a tab, a tap, a pinch) runs the same transaction (`levelChange`), so the underline moves with it. Changes the user did not make (the minute turning) animate only without Reduce Motion; with it, zooms, settles and slides go and fades stay. A Mac keeps to the system's table and list animations; what the pointer drives (a chart's day, the detail beside a table) changes at once. To look at a change, record the simulator (`xcrun simctl io booted recordVideo`) and read its frames (ffmpeg `mpdecimate`), before and after.
- Every LanguageModelSession lives in `Extractor` (ingest) or `DayPlanner` (the day plan), and so does every use of `ClaudeClient`. Log usage on every call (it is; see `UsageRecord`).
- Claude is the target model (`IngestOptions.RoutePolicy.claude` + `IngestOptions.claude`, the user's own key from Settings, Sonnet 5.5 by default; Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 the others, the cases named by line (`.sonnet`), a stored older id read as its line's current one, `ClaudeModelChoice(stored:)`): the whole document in one call (60k-token chunks), every PDF page attached as a picture beside its text so tables are read as printed, a picture transcribed first (`ClaudeSchemas.pageReading`, read back by `PageReadingJSON`) so the guards have text to check against. The device model (`.onDevice`) is the user's choice in Settings, the small tasks' model (the day plan, unless `planWithClaude`), and the fallback for one document once the user confirms it (`SourceDocument.readOnDevice`, set by `HorizonStore.readOnDevice`). Claude is reached with the user's key or through Horizon's reading service (`AppModel.claudeAccess`, Settings' Claude section, DECISIONS 2026-10-23 · The reading service in the app): Claude for someone with no key, the service's model (Sonnet 5.5) only, App Attest in place of an account (`ReadingService`), offered on iOS with App Attest and the default there for a device with no key stored; Settings, the intents and the purpose strings say "through Horizon's server" where they would say "under your key". Its refusals are classes of their own (`ClaudeError.allowanceUsed`, `.deviceUnknown` (a new key once), `.deviceRefused`, `.serviceBusy`, `.serviceUnavailable`), said as "Horizon's reading service …", never as a key's, and stop the drain, since every document from the device gets the same answer. Claude that cannot be reached (`IngestError.claudeUnavailable`: no key, a rejected key, no connection, a rate limit, an overload) leaves the document `awaitingCloudParse` with the reason; nothing falls back on its own. The client retries what can pass (dropouts, rate limits, overloads, 5xx) and never what cannot (key, model, request, an account out of credit, a model the key may not use: each in the user's words, and each stops the drain, since it stops every document behind it); a request too large is sent again with smaller pictures, then splits the chunk, each half with its own pages. The stream is split on line feeds only (an SSE event may carry U+2028 in a quoted policy). Network paths are tested with `ClaudeTransport` scripts, never the network. Reading asks for no thinking (`ClaudeModelChoice.thinkingOff`: `thinking: between_tools` to Sonnet 5.5, where `disabled` is a 400, and `disabled` to Haiku 5.5, each at high or below; Opus 5.5 cannot be kept from thinking, so it reads at low effort instead): with thinking on, a reply sometimes came back an empty structure, and the read cost four times the tokens. The plan and the calendar step think adaptively, at low effort. The API's Foundation Models package (`ClaudeForFoundationModels`) is not used: see DECISIONS 2026-09-14.
- Claude's schemas are hand-written (`ClaudeSchemas`) with properties in the order the model should write them and optional keys simply not required: the API enforces the schema's property order on structured output, and a model that skips optional keys and then wants one back out of order is forced into garbage. Never let a schema reach the wire through a dictionary.
- Claude's reply begins with an outline (the name of every item, and the counts of grade rows, policies and breaks) before any of them: the model takes the measure of the whole document first, and the reading bar is exact from then on. `PartialJSON.Snapshot.writing` names the key being written, `ExtractionJSON.progress` turns that into a `ReadProgress` (stage, done, total, the current item's name), the extractor sends `IngestEvent.progress` whenever it changes (the stage before the rows, so the rows are measured against its total), and `ReadingProgress` gives each stage a band of the part and a caption ("What's due · 12 of 18 · Quiz 8"). The outline's names stand in as rows (no date) until each item is written, under the same ids. The device model outlines nothing: its stages have no totals, its rows are the measure, and the bar creeps. The outline bought no measurable accuracy on the fixtures (DECISIONS 2026-09-15); it is there for the bar.
- The reading line is one line, always: `ReadingProgress.captions` gives each stage's wordings widest first (with the item's name, without it, the count alone), `lines` puts the part before them, and `ReadingSection` shows the first that fits (`ViewThatFits`). A new wording stays short (the test holds every one without an item's name to 28 characters) and comes with a narrower one after it.
- A document's name for the user is `displayName`: its `title`, else its file's name tidied (`DocumentName`). The title is the device model's small task whatever reads the document (`Extractor.title`, asked through `IngestOptions.titleRequest`, arriving as `IngestEvent.named`): beside Claude's read, before the device's own, after the first part for a picture (from Claude's transcript, or a digest of what was read). A title is kept only when every word of its own is in what it was named from (the document's first 1,500 characters or so, a picture's transcript or a digest of what was read) or the file's name. `title == nil` means nobody has tried (the coordinator names such documents a few at a time, `nameUntitled`), empty means the model had none, and a title on file stands. Never show `filename` to the user except as the detail under the title in Documents.
- A Claude title stands (it names the item as printed, or composes it: "Lab 1 report"); one whose significant words the document never prints is the model's own and goes to review (`Source.containsWords(of:)`). The device model's titles are still renamed to the document's wording.
- The day plan is kept for its day only, night included (UserDefaults `dayPlan`; `DayPlan.kept`: until 4 AM after the day, or while a block of it is still on or asking, then dropped); the user's plan instructions (`planInstructions`) persist and go into every plan prompt. The plan by the numbers cannot read them. Nothing of the plan reaches Claude unless the user turned on "Plan the day with Claude": a device without a model then plans by the numbers. "x of y done" counts work and study blocks only (`DayPlan.workBlocks`); Tonight Until offers only the night's ends still ahead, and an end chosen while a plan is being made plans again once it lands. The Live Activity's buttons update the activity before their intent returns, and an activity the user removed is not started again for the same plan. The plan organises the day and holds nobody to it: a block can be marked done (`PlanBlock.completedAt`) or set aside (`skippedAt`: Skip before or while it is on, "Not Finished" once it is over), each from a leading swipe, the context menu, the editor or the Live Activity (Done; Skip, or Not Yet once the block is over), and done from the dot as well; marking one clears the other, neither is an edit (Plan Again does not ask about them), and neither touches Reminders (Reminders owns an item's completion; a block is a stretch of the day, not the item). A work block that ends open asks "finished?" (`DayPlan.awaitingAnswer`) for `DayPlan.answerWindow` (15 minutes): Done or Not Yet in the Live Activity, the accent ring and "Finished?" kicker on its row in Now. Unanswered, it is taken as not finished: nothing is recorded (open is where every block starts) and the question goes. Meals, rests and calendar events ask nothing when they end. The plan streams on every route: `DayPlanner.plan(progress:)` reports the complete blocks validated so far (a prefix of the final plan, calendar events included up to the last block), `AppModel.planDraft` keeps block identities across updates, and one `PlanList` shows the draft and then the plan, so rows carry over without re-animating.
- The day's hours are the user's (`PlanHours`, whole hours after the day's midnight): a start from 5 AM to noon and an end from 6 PM to 4 AM, where an end past 24 is that night (26 is 2 AM). The usual hours are Tell the Planner's "Your day" (`AppModel.usualPlanHours`, 8 AM to 10 PM by default); tonight's end is Now's "Tonight until" pop-up (`TonightUntil`: on iOS at the foot of the timetable and on the invitation; on the Mac in the column beside the page, `NowRail`, and Plan ▸ Tonight Until), for that night only (`AppModel.tonight`, then the usual again: Health's one-time sleep change, not its schedule). Choosing it plans again when there is a plan, asking first when the plan was edited by hand (`chooseTonightEnd`, `pendingTonightEnd`, `planAgain`); a plan made after the day's end says so and points at it ("The day ended at 10 PM. To keep going tonight, choose a later time below."). The small hours belong to the night before (`PlanHours.day(of:)`: before 4 AM, the latest end): a plan made at 00:30 is for the day the night began, its prompt says "after midnight", the calendar is read to the night's end (`CalendarSource.busy(in:)`), and a model's "00:30" (or the block editor's) is placed that night by `PlanHours.span`, an end before its start crossing midnight only within six hours.
- The day plan reads the calendar before it plans (`DayPlanner.readCalendar`, a step of its own on the plan's route, low effort on Claude): each event is coursework time (a study session, study group, library time, "lock in") or fixed, and coursework time names its course when the event names a subject (a math study session is for Calculus II; library time is for any course). Fixed events stay hard stops. Coursework time is time to fill, with obligations of its course only, never past its edges (`DayPlanner.windows`: the free day plus each coursework event whole, even outside the day's hours and past midnight, cut at every edge, hard stops still hard inside it); a block there carries the event's name (`PlanBlock.within`, shown under its title in Now), and the event itself leaves the timetable when filled. The model decides what an obligation is: an item that happens at a set time (a test, exam, midterm, in-class quiz, presentation) gets `PlanBlock.Kind.study` blocks for preparing, titled "Study for …" (or the model's own short framing), with a reason that never says due or finish (one that does is replaced by the date and weight: `saysDue`); anything handed in is `work`. - Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight and the system reach Horizon through App Intents (M6, DECISIONS 2026-10-05 · App Intents), and Siri's own model acts only through what adopts a schema, plus the App Shortcuts' phrases (WWDC26: custom intents are Shortcuts' and Spotlight's, never among Siri's tools): an item is a reminder (`.reminders.reminder`), a course a list (`.reminders.list`), a grade row a section, a policy a note (`.notes.note`) in its course's folder under one account. So "for Calc, add Quiz 4 on Thursday", "push it to Sunday" and "what's the makeup policy for Calc" need no app name. The schema intents (`createReminder`, `updateReminder`, `deleteReminders`, `createList`) are Horizon's only adds, changes and deletes for Siri; `.system.open` (one type per app: items) and `.system.searchInApp` take the rest; what no schema covers is an App Shortcut (`HorizonShortcuts`: at most ten, Apple's two to five for the key features; by importance, every phrase naming the app, at most one parameter, an enum's becoming one shortcut per value). A schema's parameters Horizon has no use for stay optional, and a collection among them takes `@Parameter(default: [])` (an image one also `supportedContentTypes`): without the default, Siri asks the user for it (AppIntentsTesting caught `tags`). A schema intent may take optional parameters of its own (Add Item's Site, Every, Until, First Number; Change Item's Counts Toward, Site, Skipped, Review): Siri ignores them, and Shortcuts shows only what the intent's parameter summary lists, which without one of the app's is the schema's own, its fields alone, so every schema intent with parameters of its own declares a `parameterSummary` that lists them (it hid them all once, seen in the simulator's Shortcuts). A schema intent also takes Horizon's words for its title (`static let title`: "Change Item", not "Update Reminder") and its fields' labels (`@Parameter(title:)`: "Item", "Course", "Done", "Description"); the generated Find actions and an entity's type name keep the schema's ("Find Reminder"), since the build takes no override for them. The schema has no field for an item's grade row or site, so Siri reads and sets both as its tags (`ItemTags`: a tag naming a site sets the site, one naming a grade row of its course the row, any other is said to be neither and left off), and Update Reminder runs once per target, so "tag all of Calc's homework WebAssign" changes each. An entity a schema intent takes has a query that finds it by words (`EntityStringQuery`), or the build's metadata validation fails.
