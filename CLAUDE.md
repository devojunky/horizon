# Horizon

Native iOS 27 / macOS 27 app: an intake engine that turns course documents into obligations and writes them to EventKit. Claude (the Messages API, the user's own key) reads the documents; the device model takes the small tasks and is the confirmed fallback. Spec: `SPEC.md`. Deviations and choices: `DECISIONS.md`.

## Working together
The owner (whoever writes the prompts; "the user" elsewhere in this file is the app's user) brings the ideas and the product judgement. You bring the technical knowledge, and on that front you know more than they do. Work from that split:
- Review every suggestion, addition, change and feature proposal in detail before building it, and review it against what the owner is after, not the wording of one prompt. Ask: what does the owner want, and will this get them there and keep them there? Their goals for the product outrank the intent of a single message.
- Act in the owner's interest. A request that would undermine their own aims (a shortcut that costs later, a feature that fights the spec's premise, a choice made without knowing what the platform offers or forbids) gets a case for the better path, made plainly with the reasons: you are expected to sway them when your judgement is sound, not to comply and note a concern.
- Refine their ideas and propose your own. Make the calls they would not think to make for want of technical knowledge or awareness of what the platform can do, and take initiative on their behalf. Say what was decided for them and why, so the decision stays theirs to reverse.
- A case the owner has heard and turned down is settled: build what they asked. Do not manufacture objections either; a sound idea gets built, and the review exists to catch what they cannot see, not to have an opinion on everything.

xcrun simctl launch booted com.apple.Preferences                               # sends Horizon to the background (a read under way goes on until iOS's grant runs out, ~30 s, then pauses); launching Horizon again relaunches it and the read resumes from its checkpoint

The simulator runs a 300M model, not the 3B one; use it to exercise the pipeline, not to judge extraction quality. A launch's arguments are sometimes not applied on this simulator (the process the system already has is fronted instead): the drain's log line names the route and the key's length, so check it before trusting a `--claude-key` or `--read-on-device` run. BGTaskScheduler does not exist on the simulator. Settings → Debug → "Ingest Sample Syllabus" (DEBUG builds) feeds `Apps/Horizon/Samples/sample-syllabus.pdf`.

## Rules of the codebase
- The user never types a task (SPEC §2). No composer UI, ever.
- The look is an editorial page (DECISIONS 2026-09-05): every screen sits on `Design.paper` (`.paper()` on lists and forms, and `.onPaper()` on every section or row of a plain list: row modifiers do nothing on the List itself, and a row left out paints a white or black cell over the paper), titles are serif (`Design.installAppearance`, `.deck()`, `.display()`, `.numeral()`), sections get a `Kicker`, rules are hairlines, and `Design.accent` means weight or urgency only. Empty states are `EditorialEmpty`; something in progress is `WorkingLine`. Motion is earned: rows arriving, the level crossfade, the underline sliding, the "now" dot breathing; all of it respects Reduce Motion.
- Every LanguageModelSession lives in `Extractor` (ingest) or `DayPlanner` (the day plan), and so does every use of `ClaudeClient`. Log usage on every call (it is; see `UsageRecord`).
- Claude is the target model (`IngestOptions.RoutePolicy.claude` + `IngestOptions.claude`, the user's own key from Settings, Sonnet 5 by default): the whole document in one call (60k-token chunks), every PDF page attached as a picture beside its text so tables are read as printed, a picture transcribed first (`PageReading`) so the guards have text to check against. The device model (`.onDevice`) is the user's choice in Settings, the small tasks' model (the day plan, unless `planWithClaude`), and the fallback for one document once the user confirms it (`SourceDocument.readOnDevice`, set by `HorizonStore.readOnDevice`). Claude that cannot be reached (`IngestError.claudeUnavailable`: no key, a rejected key, no connection, a rate limit, an overload) leaves the document `awaitingCloudParse` with the reason; nothing falls back on its own. The client retries what can pass (dropouts, rate limits, overloads, 5xx) and never what cannot (key, model, request); a request too large splits the chunk. Network paths are tested with `ClaudeTransport` scripts, never the network. No thinking is requested for reading. The API's Foundation Models package (`ClaudeForFoundationModels`) is not used: see DECISIONS 2026-09-14.
- Claude's schemas are hand-written (`ClaudeSchemas`) with properties in the order the model should write them and optional keys simply not required: the API enforces the schema's property order on structured output, and a model that skips optional keys and then wants one back out of order is forced into garbage. Never let a schema reach the wire through a dictionary.
- Claude's reply begins with an outline (the name of every item, and the counts of grade rows, policies and breaks) before any of them: the model takes the measure of the whole document first, and the reading bar is exact from then on. `PartialJSON.Snapshot.writing` names the key being written, `ExtractionJSON.progress` turns that into a `ReadProgress` (stage, done, total, the current item's name), the extractor sends `IngestEvent.progress` whenever it changes (the stage before the rows, so the rows are measured against its total), and `ReadingProgress` gives each stage a band of the part and a caption ("What's due · Quiz 8 · 12 of 18"). The outline's names stand in as rows (no date) until each item is written, under the same ids. The device model outlines nothing: its stages have no totals, its rows are the measure, and the bar creeps. The outline bought no measurable accuracy on the fixtures (DECISIONS 2026-09-15); it is there for the bar.
- A Claude title stands (it names the item as printed, or composes it: "Lab 1 report"); one whose significant words the document never prints is the model's own and goes to review (`Source.containsWords(of:)`). The device model's titles are still renamed to the document's wording.
- The day plan is kept for the day only (UserDefaults `dayPlan`, dropped once the day ends); the user's plan instructions (`planInstructions`) persist and go into every plan prompt. The plan by the numbers cannot read them. A block can be marked done (`PlanBlock.completedAt`; the dot, a leading swipe, the context menu, the editor, or the Live Activity's Done button); done is not an edit, so Plan Again does not ask about it, and it never touches Reminders (Reminders owns an item's completion; a block is a stretch of the day, not the item). The plan's Live Activity (`PlanActivityController`, iOS) mirrors `DayPlan.focus(at:)`: started when something is ahead, updated after every change the app makes and on Now's minute tick, its stale date the current block's end, ended two hours after the last block is done; force-quitting the app ends it, as iOS does for every app. The intent runs in the app: `PlanCommands.markDone` is set in `AppModel.init`. The plan streams on every route: `DayPlanner.plan(progress:)` reports the complete blocks validated so far (a prefix of the final plan, calendar events included up to the last block), `AppModel.planDraft` keeps block identities across updates, and one `PlanList` shows the draft and then the plan, so rows carry over without re-animating.
- The strip's content follows the zoom: `Strip.level(daysAcross:)` decides weeks / days / items; load is colour (`Strip.heat`), never size.
- Dates: the model emits `dateAsWritten` (copied text) and `dueDate` (ISO). `DateResolver` re-parses the wording; the wording wins on disagreement (a model year slip is forgiven; a model date the document also names means review), except that wording naming several dates without "due" lets the model choose among them. TBD wording rejects a model date outright. A date that neither the wording nor the document mentions is dropped (text pages) or sent to review (image pages). A date printed before the item on a row with a later date is `dateAmbiguous` → review.
- Year: term label, else `IngestOptions.defaultYear` / `--year`, else today. The data detector's own year guess is never used. The detector reads "due <date>" as a span from today to the date and returns nothing once the date is past, so `DateResolver.candidate` writes "due:" before running it (tests that name dates near today are the canary: they broke the day after Sep 9).
- Date-only deadlines are stored at 23:59 local with `dueHasTime == false`.
- Obligations below `IngestOptions.reviewThreshold` (0.7) are `.needsReview`; TBD dates are `.awaitingDate`. Nothing is silently dropped except items unsupported by the document text (counted in `warnings`).
- Prompt or schema changes must be followed by an eval run. Pass = mean recall ≥ 0.9 and zero `.ready` errors (spurious or wrong-dated items that would ship without review); errors that land in review are reported, not fatal.
- Provenance: every obligation carries `sourcePage`; policies carry `verbatim`; same-title-two-dates items carry `conflictingDate`.
- The deterministic guards check the model's facts against the document's own words: a PDF's text layer for a text chunk, and for a picture the transcript Claude wrote of it (`ExtractionOutcome.transcript`, lenient: review, never drop). A picture the device model read has no transcript and its items go to review. There is no OCR of our own any more.
- Sites (platforms) come only from the document's words (`Merger.Source.platforms(in:)`: product names as whole words, the ambiguous ones capitalised; a publisher is not a site) or, for a picture with no such words, the model's reading of it. A grading row's site rides on its component (`GradeComponent.platform`). Coverage's "from which nothing has ever been read" stands on live items and components, never on `Course.platforms`, and a site read from any picture counts as read.
- Keep the schema small: it is sent with every chunk on a 4096-token on-device context.
- Storage: SwiftData in the App Group, CloudKit-shaped from V1 (optional/defaulted fields, no unique attributes, optional relationships with inverses). Soft deletes only (`deletedAt`) for obligations, courses and documents. A deleted course or document goes to Recently Deleted (`Bin`, 30 days) with what its deletion took, stamped with the same instant (`Bin.wentWith`), and a restore brings back exactly that: a tombstone the user made before stays deleted. A tombstone that went with its source is `retiredWithSource` and does not capture a re-read item (a user's own deletion does). Erasing (`Delete Now`, or the bin expiring in `expireBin`) is for good: a document is hard-deleted with its file, a course stays an `erasedAt` tombstone until the purge. A binned document shared again is read afresh (revived, queued, its tombstones retired); a document naming a binned or erased course revives it the same way. Never confirm a deletion that lands in the bin; only erasing asks. `HorizonStore` is the only writer; `ReminderProjector` is the only Reminders writer and re-fetches the Horizon list before every write.
- Field ownership: documents own title/due/platform/component unless the field is in `userOverrides`; Reminders owns completion; hand edits (review editor, manual entry, edits made in Reminders) set overrides. A re-ingest that changes an owned field goes back to review with a `changeNote`.
- Manual entry is a fallback reached from the overflow menu, never a primary action; such items carry `origin = manual`.
- A read survives the app leaving. Every finished part (a text chunk or an image page) is kept as an `IngestCheckpoint` on the document (`SourceDocument.checkpoint`), and the next read of that document, on the same route with the same chunking, goes on from there, replaying the kept parts into the reading bar and the provisional list (`IngestEvent.started(resumed:)`, "picking up at part n of m"); a checkpoint from another route or chunking is set aside. On iOS every drain holds a background-time assertion, so a read goes on after the user leaves (a cloud read usually finishes; the on-device model works too, though it may rate-limit a background app, which is treated as `modelNotReady`: queued, kept, tried again); when iOS's grant runs out the read is paused, not cancelled (`AppModel.paused`, the document stays queued with its checkpoint, "Queued · n of m parts read" in Documents), a `BGProcessingTask` is booked (with the network when the route is Claude), and the read resumes the moment the app is active again or in that task. Force-quit or a crash lose only the part under way. The user's Cancel is still a cancellation (`.cancelled`, Retry in Documents).
- Several documents can feed one course: `enqueueDocument(courseID:)` files a document, the coordinator hands the parser `IngestOptions.knownCourse` from `HorizonStore.knownCourse(for:)`, and `assignDocument` files a loose one later. Points at stake are recomputed per course in the store (`PointsAtStake.perItem`, shared with the merger).
- Courses are shown by `displayName` (title, else code) everywhere, including reminder titles. Hand edits to a course (`HorizonStore.updateCourse`) set `Course.userOverrides`, which `upsertCourse` respects. Any obligation can be edited (`ObligationEditor` `.edit`) or deleted (soft delete); both go through the same overrides as review edits.
- The reading route is one choice in Settings (`AppModel.readingRoute`: Claude / on this device → `IngestCoordinator.setRoute` + `setClaude`); a key entered turns Claude on, a key removed turns it off. A document waiting for Claude is announced on Coverage with Try Again and "Read on This Device…", which asks first (Documents and the Mac's table offer the same). The More menu holds pages and actions only (Documents, Settings, Add Item, Add Course, Edit Courses), and the page pulled down is the sync.
- The app icon is an Icon Composer document (`AppIcon.icon`, HIG Liquid Glass): a full-bleed automatic gradient of the paper, then foreground groups the system lights, refracts and shadows (the sun, translucent glass; the horizon and its marks, ink with a dark-appearance specialization to cream). Layers are unmasked 1024 SVGs with hard edges and no baked effects; the flat appiconset is only for OS 25 and earlier. `options.fileTypes.icon` in project.yml makes xcodegen add the document as one file (`wrapper.icon`) in the Resources phase, where actool picks it up with `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_APPICON_NAME`.
- The Mac is its own shape (`MacShell.swift`): a sidebar (Coverage, Horizon, Now; Review and Recently Deleted badged, Documents), Coverage as stories beside the selected course, tables for Documents and the bin (sortable, context menus, the Delete key), every action in the menu bar with its shortcut (⌘O add, ⌘N item, ⇧⌘N course, ⌘R sync, ⌘1–6 pages, ⌘E / ⌘⌫ course, ⇧⌘P plan, ⌥⌘T planner), Settings at ⌘, as a two-pane window, files dropped from the Finder anywhere on the window, editor sheets with Cancel / Save at the foot (Escape / Return). Row modifiers: `.onPaper()` sets no row background on macOS, so lists keep their selection highlight. The Mac build is signed with the development identity so its TCC grants survive rebuilds; the document store falls back to Application Support when the App Group folder cannot be made.
